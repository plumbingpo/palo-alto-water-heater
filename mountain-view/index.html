When Your Water Heater Fails in Mountain View

When your hot water quits, how fast help shows up matters more than anything else. That's why we keep scheduling flexible across the area and put a real person on the phone. Urgent calls usually get a technician within a day, and we treat leaks and no-hot-water calls as top priority.

The emergency calls we run usually follow a pattern. On gas units, it's a pilot that keeps going out, a burner that lights and drops out, or a failed gas valve. On electric heaters, it's a burned-out element, a faulty high-limit switch, or a breaker that won't stay on. And on any type, it's leaks at the base, the drain valve, or the fittings, plus relief valves that discharge. Because our trucks carry thermocouples, elements, thermostats, gas valves, relief valves, and fittings, most of these get fixed on the first trip, with the price agreed before any work begins.

Two common situations call for a quick step from you before a technician arrives. The first is a leak: shut the cold water valve above the tank and shut off the power or gas, which keeps the tank from refilling and heating. The second is a relief valve that discharges or a tank that makes loud banging noises: both point to excess temperature or pressure, so shut the heater down and keep away from the discharge pipe. If you smell gas at any point, leave the house and call your gas utility before anything else. After that, call us at (650) 220-1790. That small step can save you from a far bigger repair.

Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Mountain View

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year in hard-water areas.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or a damp spot under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A tank that's just begun to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number on your phone now so you can reach us fast as soon as something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

If you could see inside your water heater, the case for maintenance would make itself. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, creates popping and rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, meant to corrode first, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. Across Palo Alto, CA, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

The work is hands-on and specific. We flush the tank until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we check the expansion tank's pressure, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

If you have a tankless unit, the routine changes but the goal is the same. Minerals coat the heat exchanger over time, reducing efficiency and eventually causing shutdowns. Our technicians connect a circulation pump to the isolation valves and run a descaling solution through the exchanger, clean the water filter, inspect the venting, and check the condensate drain on condensing models. When to Call for Water Heater Maintenance

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • Small drips at the drain valve or connections
  • Rumbling, popping, or crackling from the tank as it heats
  • A smell like rotten eggs that shows up only on the hot side
  • Less hot water available than when the tank was new
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • Temperature swings or reduced flow from a tankless heater
  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use

Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Maintenance?

A few maintenance tasks suit homeowners, and many do not. Keeping clutter away from the heater, checking for leaks, and noticing new noises are all useful. Flushing, anode replacement, relief valve testing, and burner or element work are a different matter. Hot water, seized fittings, brittle drain valves, and sediment that clogs the valve midway through can turn a simple job into a flooded floor. Relief valves on older heaters sometimes fail to close after testing. And gas-side checks involve flame quality and venting, which protect your household's air.

Our technicians carry the tools and parts for every step: replacement drain valves, anode rods sized for your tank, flexible anodes for tight clearances, T&P valves, and meters for testing elements and thermostats. For tankless systems, we bring the pump and descaling setup. Just as important, we know what to look for, from early corrosion at the fittings to a thermocouple starting to weaken, so you can plan a repair before it becomes a failure. The goal is simple: a heater that lasts longer, runs more efficiently, and does not surprise you with a leak.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Do not store boxes, paint, or chemicals against the water heater
  • Keep the thermostat near 120 degrees to limit scalding and slow sediment buildup
  • Replace a heavily consumed anode rod to extend the tank's life
  • Ask about water treatment if flushing turns up heavy mineral buildup
  • Schedule a flush every year, or every six months if you have hard water
  • Plan yearly descaling for a tankless heater, sooner if scale warnings appear
  • Look around the base of the heater periodically for moisture

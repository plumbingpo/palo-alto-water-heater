A wet floor beside the water heater is a call we hear about constantly, and the first question is always the same: where is it actually coming from? Water travels along pipes and collects wherever the floor dips, so the puddle is often nowhere near the source. It might be the temperature and pressure relief valve or its discharge line, the drain valve near the bottom of the tank, the inlet and outlet fittings on top, condensation on a cold tank, or a nearby pipe that has nothing to do with the heater. It might also be the tank itself, and that answer changes everything. In Palo Alto, CA, we trace the source before we touch a wrench.

Finding the source takes a few careful steps. We clear and dry the area, then watch where new water appears. The relief valve and its discharge pipe get checked first, followed by the drain valve and the inlet and outlet fittings, where dielectric unions and flex connectors commonly fail. If the heater is gas-fired, we look inside the burner compartment, since water there can be condensation or a sign the tank has given out. When the relief valve is involved, we measure water pressure and check whether expansion has anywhere to go. We recommend nothing until the source is confirmed.

Most leaks are fixed in a single visit. We replace worn relief valves, swap plastic drain valves for sturdier brass valves, rebuild corroded connections, add or recharge an expansion tank, and install a pressure-reducing valve when street pressure is the real culprit. If the tank itself is leaking, there is no safe patch, and we will tell you that plainly along with your replacement options. We also help prevent the next leak from doing damage: a drain pan piped to a proper drain, a water sensor or automatic shutoff valve, and a clear label on the shutoff. When to Call for Water Heater Leak Repair

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Water stains, soft flooring, or musty smells near the heater closet
  • Higher water use with no obvious explanation
  • A T&P valve line that drips or releases water now and then
  • Moisture or rust flakes under the burner access cover
  • Rust, green corrosion, or white crust on the fittings at the top of the tank
  • Rusty or discolored hot water paired with dampness at the base
  • Water collecting under or beside the heater
  • Steady drips from the drain valve near the base

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Leaks look simple and often are not. Water can start at a top fitting, run down under the jacket, and show up at the base, and condensation on cold tanks add confusion. The risks go beyond a wrong part. Plugging a dripping relief valve removes the protection against excess pressure and heat, forcing a corroded connection can split it open, and electric heaters bring high-voltage wiring into a wet area. And if the tank itself is failing, no DIY fix will hold for long.

Our technicians trace leaks methodically and fix the cause, not just the drip. We dry the unit, let new water reveal the source, test water pressure when the relief valve is involved, and check for expansion issues that cause weeping valves. We replace valves and fittings with durable replacements, swap plastic drain valves for brass, and add an expansion tank or pressure-reducing valve when pressure is the root problem. If the tank has failed, we tell you directly and explain replacement options, including the drain pan and leak protection that can keep the next failure from soaking your floors.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • A properly drained pan under the tank catches small leaks before they reach your floors
  • Inspecting the anode rod periodically is one of the best ways to delay a tank leak
  • Add a floor sensor or an automatic shutoff valve that closes the supply when it detects water
  • Make a habit of checking around the heater for moisture or corrosion every few weeks
  • An expansion tank that is present and properly charged keeps the relief valve from opening every cycle
  • Check your water pressure; if it runs above about 80 psi, a pressure-reducing valve protects the tank and valves
  • Flush the tank once a year so sediment does not overheat the bottom and speed corrosion

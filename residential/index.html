Upfront Pricing

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Nobody likes surprises on the final bill. That's why Palo Alto Water Heater quotes every job upfront, after a full diagnostic that covers the heater's age, fuel type, and capacity, the burner assembly or heating elements, the anode rod and relief valve, and the vent and gas connections. You'll know what's wrong, what the fix involves, and what it costs before you say yes.

A lot of water heater failures give quiet warnings long before the tank quits. Watch for discolored or rusty hot water, popping noises when the burner fires, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, or showers that go cold sooner than they used to. Behind those symptoms are usually heavy sediment, a depleted anode rod, or a worn dip tube. A quick inspection at the first sign usually costs far less than cleaning up after a failed tank.

Not every call is an emergency. Many homeowners ask us to evaluate an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with annual descaling), replacing on your own schedule beats a rushed swap after a leak. We'll give you an honest read on how much life your equipment has left.

Annual Flushing

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

A maintenance visit from Palo Alto Water Heater is more than a quick drain and refill. Our technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, lifts and tests the relief valve, inspects the burner, flue, and draft on gas models, checks elements and thermostats on electric models, and sets a safe water temperature. Minor faults are caught while they're inexpensive to correct, and the heater stays running clean and efficient.

Every maintenance plan we write is a little different. A tank in its later years needs a closer look than a new heater, a tankless unit on hard water needs annual descaling to keep its heat exchanger working, and a large family cycles the heater far harder than a couple does. We set up each visit around your equipment and water, so every service helps keep the unit efficient and protects the equipment you've already paid for.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a regular maintenance schedule pays off. A clean tank heats water faster and more quietly, a healthy anode rod keeps rust from attacking the steel, and a working relief valve is ready to vent pressure if something goes wrong. Most families find that annual service costs far less than replacing a water heater even a few years early.

Proper Sizing

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

When it comes to water heaters, bigger is not automatically better. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, the number of gallons of hot water it can deliver in a busy hour, while for a tankless unit it's the GPM it can sustain when the incoming water is cold. That's why we never just copy the gallon rating of the old heater. We look at how many people live in the home, the number of bathrooms, and big draws like a soaking tub or a high-capacity washer.

Choosing a new water heater comes down to more than tank size. Our technician will compare tankless units against a traditional tank, and gas against electric or hybrid, laying out operating costs, venting and gas line work, electrical service, and the installation itself. Tankless works well for many households, though it often requires a bigger gas line and new venting, and a well-sized tank can be the simpler, less expensive choice. The decision is yours, based on your home and budget.

Common Problems

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

Homes around Palo Alto run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Palo Alto Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that never gets hot enough, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks with burned-out elements, rumbling tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

The correct fix depends entirely on what actually failed. On electric tanks, that usually means an element or thermostat. On gas units, it tends to be a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure problems are solved with a thermal expansion tank and a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and leaks at the drain valve or fittings get repaired before the damage spreads. We also check the temperature setting, since water above 120°F can scald young children and older adults in seconds. If the tank itself is leaking, no repair will hold, and we'll say so plainly. Any gas smell means leave the house and call the gas utility right away.

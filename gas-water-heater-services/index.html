Most gas water heaters in Palo Alto, CA work the same basic way. Gas enters through a control valve that doubles as the thermostat and feeds a burner beneath the tank, and the hot exhaust travels up a flue through the middle of the tank and out through the vent. A thermocouple, or a flame sensor on newer electronic models, tells the valve a flame is present; without that signal, the valve shuts off the gas for safety. The design is dependable, but it has a few predictable weak spots. Thermocouples wear out, pilot tubes and burner ports clog, the flame arrestor screen at the base packs up with lint and dust, control valves fail, and vents lose their draft.

Palo Alto Water Heater does not guess with gas appliances. On every Palo Alto call, our technicians test the thermocouple's millivolt output, inspect the pilot and burner flame, check gas pressure at the valve, and run a draft test at the draft hood to confirm exhaust is actually leaving the house. We look inside the combustion chamber for scorching, rust, and signs of flame rollout, which tell us whether the flue is blocked or the burner is misbehaving, and we check for carbon monoxide around the heater. One rule comes before all of it: if you smell gas, do not light anything or flip switches. Leave the house, call your gas utility from outside, and then call us once it is safe.

Once we know the cause, the repair is usually simple. A new thermocouple or flame sensor, a cleaned pilot tube and burner, a cleared flame arrestor screen, or a replacement gas control valve gets most heaters running again the same day. Vent problems, like a disconnected connector or a flue pipe without enough rise, are corrected on the spot. If sediment is rumbling in the tank, we flush it, and if the anode rod is spent, we can replace it. Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • A pilot light that will not stay lit
  • A vent pipe that has separated, rusted, or left scorch marks near the top of the heater
  • Leaks at the drain valve, the fittings, or the bottom of the tank
  • No hot water at all, with the burner never firing
  • A flame that looks orange or floppy rather than steady and blue
  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • A tank that pops and bangs while it heats, a classic sign of sediment
  • A rotten-egg odor near the water heater, in which case get out and call the gas company first

The Benefits of Professional Gas Water Heater Services

Two things make gas water heater work a poor DIY project: the parts involve fuel and combustion, and the symptoms overlap. A pilot outage might come from a worn thermocouple, a partly blocked pilot tube, lint on the flame arrestor screen, a downdraft, or an internal valve failure. A wrong guess there just wastes money. Guess wrong on a gas fitting or vent connection and the stakes are much higher, since a small leak or a spillage problem can put gas or carbon monoxide into the living space. Gas valve and heater replacements also typically call for a permit.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
  • Make sure the vent pipe stays connected, sloped upward, and free of rust
  • Install carbon monoxide alarms on every level of the home and test them monthly
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years and replaced when it is worn
  • Never store flammable liquids near a gas water heater
  • Choose a setting of about 120 degrees to balance comfort, safety, and efficiency
  • Clean dust from around the base so the burner gets plenty of combustion air

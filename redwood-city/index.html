Keeping Your Redwood City Water Heater Running Longer

Most early failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard to protect the heat exchanger.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or moisture under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and causes far less stress than an emergency swap after a leak.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number on your phone now so you can reach us fast the moment something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

A water heater in Redwood City rarely fails without warning.

Keeping Your Santa Clara Water Heater Running Longer

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or a damp spot under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A tank that's just begun to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts today so help is one call away the moment something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Common Water Heater Problems in Santa Clara

Water heaters in and around Santa Clara face a familiar set of problems. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, hard water may be part of the picture: calcium and magnesium settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, which produces the popping and rumbling many homeowners hear during a heating cycle. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, which is why annual descaling is worth keeping up with in hard-water areas.

Age is the other big factor. Plenty of homes around here still have a tank that was installed a decade or more ago, and beyond about ten years, the anode rod is usually spent and corrosion begins attacking the steel from the inside. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, so our technicians handle everything from worn thermocouples and sooty burners to burned-out elements and failed thermostats.

Code is another piece. Swapping out an older heater frequently means bringing the install up to current code: an expansion tank if the plumbing is a closed system, a relief valve drain line piped to an approved spot, venting that drafts properly on gas units, plus straps and a pan where required. Homes on well water have their own issues, such as heavier sediment, iron staining, and the rotten-egg smell that sometimes develops when the water reacts with a standard anode rod. All of these can be fixed, and most cost far less when caught early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a diagnostic visit from our team catches the small problem before it becomes an expensive one. Give us a call at (650) 220-1790 to set up a visit.
